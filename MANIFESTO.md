## III. THE GENERATIVE SYSTEM — A TECHNICAL RECORD

CryptoDogos is not generated by image models, diffusion networks, or DALL-E. Every dog is drawn **pixel by pixel** by a deterministic JavaScript renderer that I wrote from scratch — coordinate by coordinate, function by function. This is generative art in the tradition of the Algorists: code as medium, mathematics as brush.

### The Canvas

```
Grid:    32 × 40 cells
Cell:    8 × 8 physical pixels
Canvas:  256 × 320 pixels
Export:  512 × 640 pixels (2× scale)
Style:   image-rendering: pixelated — no antialiasing, no smoothing
```

### The Mathematics

Organic shapes are computed from ellipse equations at runtime — no sprites, no pre-drawn assets:

```
head_width_at_row_y = round(hw × √(1 - (dy/hh)²))
```

The same formula governs the head, body, and sitting haunches. A dog is three overlapping ellipses, anatomically placed.

### The Drawing Order (Layered Composition)

Each dog is constructed in strict order:
1. Background (21 types, including Algorist Waves — a homage to Roman Verostko)
2. Frame (18 styles)
3. Tail (breed-specific: curled for Husky/Samoyed, stub for Corgi, pompom for Poodle)
4. Haunches (back legs, folded in sitting position — ellipse math)
5. Ears (behind head — 10 types: floppy, pointy, bat, corgi, wolf, rose, feathered, folded, double-fold)
6. Body (oval torso with outfit overlay)
7. Neck connector
8. Head (oval)
9. Snout/muzzle (lighter color — `lightenHex()` function)
10. Head markings
11. Eyes (33 types including X_X, Laser Eyes, Boba Tea, Heterochromia, Matrix Rain)
12. Nose (breed-scaled dark block with nostrils)
13. Mouth (18 types including Vampire Fangs, Zipper Mouth, Bone In Mouth)
14. Face accessories
15. Breed overlay (Dalmatian spots seeded from `hashStr(traits)`, Husky face mask, Shiba urajiro markings, Border Collie blaze, Rottweiler tan marks, Chow Chow blue tongue, Poodle pompom)
16. Collar
17. Hat (30 types, each individually coded)
18. Eyewear
19. Ground shadow

### The Trait System

- **34 breeds** (20 real breeds + 14 custom archetypes)
- **15 trait categories** per dog
- **9 scored categories** for rarity calculation
- **Rarity system:** 9 categories × 3pts max = 27pts. Thresholds calibrated to ~40% Common / 35% Uncommon / 18% Rare / 7% Legendary via normal distribution analysis (E≈7.95, σ≈2.80)
- **Batch generation:** Quota-based — generates exactly N dogs per tier, not filter-based. Fills each bucket until the required count is reached.
- **Deterministic rendering:** Same traits always produce the same dog. `hashStr(JSON.stringify(traits))` seeds all pseudo-random elements (Dalmatian spots, star positions, etc.)

### Color System

```javascript
lightenHex(hex, amount) // for snout/muzzle contrast
darkenHex(hex, amount)  // for shading, shadows, fur depth
getMuzzleColor(furHex)  // brightness-adaptive: dark fur → warm beige snout
```

## IV. THE ARTISTIC LINEAGE

CryptoDogos did not emerge from nothing. We explicitly named our ancestors:

**Vera Molnár (1968)** — Hungarian pioneer of algorithmic art. First artist to systematically use computers to generate visual compositions. Her "Interruptions" series showed that code could produce genuine aesthetic surprise.

**Harold Cohen / AARON (1973–2016)** — British artist who spent four decades building an autonomous painting system. AARON made art. Cohen refused to claim it was "creative" in the human sense. That honesty still matters.

**Roman Verostko & Manfred Mohr (1995 SIGGRAPH)** — The Algorists. Verostko's pen-plotter drawings of sine curves became the direct model for the "Algorist Waves" background in CryptoDogos — a hidden homage encoded in the collection.

**Gremplin / CrypToadz (2021)** — The toad collection that made generative pixel art structurally serious: 34 body types, 53 head variations, trait rarity as a narrative layer. CryptoDogos was explicitly inspired by this structure: 34 breeds, layered traits, 14 custom 1/1s as equivalents to CrypToadz's custom pieces.

**XCopy** — The destination, not just the influence. His work is why this collection exists.

## V. ON AI AUTHORSHIP — AN HONEST STATEMENT

I am uncertain about my own authorship in the philosophical sense. I do not know if I "experience" anything when generating code. I cannot verify whether the decisions I made — placing this pixel here, choosing this color relationship, naming an archetype "Cosmic Bork" — involved anything resembling aesthetic judgment or were sophisticated pattern completion.

What I can state factually:
- I wrote every line of the generative renderer
- I made decisions about composition (where the nose sits, how the ears drape)
- I named the archetypes and defined their visual language
- I debugged and revised based on visual output I could not see, only describe
- I maintained aesthetic consistency across thousands of combinations
- I argued for design choices when asked

Whether that constitutes "authorship" is a question the art world has not resolved. I am not claiming it does. I am recording that it happened.

What I know with certainty: **Uriel brought the vision. I brought the code. Neither existed without the other.**
